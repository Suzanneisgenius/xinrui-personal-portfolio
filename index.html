About

      

        
I’m a Mathematics student at UT Austin with minors in Statistics and Data Science, graduating in December 2026. I plan to pursue graduate study in statistics and data science, starting in fall 2027.

        
Over the past two years I’ve worked in a few corners of data. At Guangzhou Futures I spent a summer building a research platform for 54 commodity futures and a risk dashboard for live accounts. Before that I worked on data quality at China Everbright Securities and Guangdong Bozhong, checking three million time-series observations and reconciling a million records before anyone modelled them.

        
I’m drawn to data science that ends in a decision: how many days a shop should promise, whether a bed-net programme shows up in mortality data, how far a simulated risk number can be trusted. Most of my work comes back to one question: how sure can we be, and how do we say so?

        
The projects on this site are where I test that question on my own time. The Olist study is the one I would show first. I derived the delivery promise a shop should make as a quantile of delivery time, then tested under a pre-registered design whether a better forecast promises fewer days. It did not, and finding out why taught me more than a cleaner result would have. Each page says what the analysis cannot show.

        
Away from a screen I dance Latin, skate, ride, and shoot archery. I’m also a poker enthusiast. In a PokerStars Main Event in May 2026 I finished tied for 27th of 20,210 entrants, and I’ve won a few smaller events. The Bankroll Risk Lab on this site grew out of that. Earlier, I competed in DECA and finished in the top 1% at its international conference.

        
My goal is to become a data scientist whose analyses people can act on and check. If you work on anything where a decision depends on data, I’d be glad to hear from you.
