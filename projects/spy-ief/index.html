Read the six-step case study

Case study reference: full active data and its calendar periods; independent of the analysis selection above. Active data: 2002-08-01 to 2026-10-02.

1

Question

  • Is the SPY–IEF daily-return correlation stable enough to treat Treasuries as a standing cushion for equities?
  • Or does one full-sample number hide periods in which the two moved together?
2

Data

  • 6,082 aligned daily returns, 2002-08-01 to 2026-10-02.
  • Source: 2024 course sample extended to 2026-10-02 with Yahoo Finance adjusted closes.
  • Joined return series: notebook-reconstructed index through 2024-07-30, then Yahoo adjusted-close returns. Levels are not original dollar prices.
  • Rows dropped by the checks: SPY 0, IEF 0; 0 dates present for only one ETF.
3

Metrics

  • Period correlation: one value from every day in a period; no window.
  • Rolling correlation at 30, 90 and 252 days, and the share of windows above zero.
  • What IEF did on the worst 5% of SPY days in each period.
  • 95% intervals from a 20-day moving-block bootstrap.
  • Walk-forward volatility forecasts scored against a persistence baseline.
4

Findings

  • Full-sample correlation −0.29 (95% interval −0.35 to −0.23).
  • 2024–2026: +0.07 (95% interval −0.05 to +0.21). Sub-periods with a positive value: 2022–2023, 2024–2026.
  • 30-day windows with a positive value: 26.1% overall, 61.8% in 2024–2026.
  • On the worst 5% of SPY days, IEF rose 78% of the time overall and 49% (17/35 days) in 2024–2026.
  • Volatility check (SPY, HAR-type rule against persistence): −11.2% MAE over 250 forecasts; −20.8% over 32 in 2024–2026.
  • Macro context, full active data: 95 eligible quarters; correlation change per +1 pp mean breakeven +0.31 (approximate 95% HAC interval +0.11 to +0.50). Excluding 2022+: +0.17 [−0.03, +0.37], 76 quarters; the estimate depends on the period. This is an association, not a cause.
5

Limits

  • Description, not explanation: no interest-rate or inflation series, so no cause is tested.
  • Two ETFs; reconstructed notebook index joined to Yahoo adjusted-close returns. Levels are not original dollar prices.
  • Period boundaries are a choice; other boundaries move the numbers.
  • Intervals depend on the 20-day block length.
  • The episode screen is exploratory and unvalidated.
6

What it means

  • One full-sample number (−0.29) would have described 2024–2026 with a different point-estimate sign (+0.07); its interval must be checked before calling a sign change stable.
  • For anyone who holds Treasuries as a cushion for equities, the practical check is period by period and on the worst equity days, not one average.
  • The same habit applies to the forecast: an average gain sits next to at least one SPY period with higher observed error; HAC intervals show whether its direction is clear.

Why 63.0% and 42.7% can both be reported for 2024–2026

The 252-day series computed on the full history has a value on 691 of the 691 days in 2024–2026; 63.0% of them are positive. Recomputing only inside that period discards the first 251 days while the window warms up, leaving 440 days, of which 42.7% are positive. On those same 440 dates the two calculations agree; the second figure is a different date denominator, not a different answer for the same dates.

What this check can and cannot say

  • Training features and completed training targets are available by each forecast origin. The future target is observed later and used only for evaluation.
  • Average error can conceal a sub-period failure, so each period is scored separately above.
  • The Python evaluation uses a Bartlett Newey–West standard error for dependent loss differences. Bandwidth is recorded; these exploratory results do not establish a lasting edge.
  • No return prediction, macro explanation or investment optimization is claimed.

Data, definitions and ownership.

The original two-person course work, the later AI-assisted extension and the reconstructed offline sample are explicitly separated.

Active data: 2024 course sample extended to 2026-10-02 with Yahoo Finance adjusted closes · 2002-08-01 to 2026-10-02 · 6,082 aligned daily returns · newer prices retrieved 2026-10-04; static until rebuilt
  • Two columns are read: Date (YYYY-MM-DD) and Adj Close. Close is never used in its place.
  • Rows with an unreadable date, a missing price, or a non-numeric or non-positive price are dropped and counted.
  • A date listed twice with one price keeps one row; with conflicting prices the whole date is dropped.
  • Only dates present in both files are used. Returns are computed after alignment; nothing is forward-filled.
  • At least 30 aligned returns are needed. Otherwise nothing is loaded and the active data stay as they are.
  • Files are read in this browser only. Nothing is uploaded to a server.

Uncertainty and limitations

Correlation intervals use moving blocks of consecutive days. The default is 20 trading days; conclusions can change with block length. A positive point estimate alone does not establish a structural break. The active data end in October 2026, contain only two ETFs. The FRED breakeven series supplies retrospective context, not causal identification. The bootstrap assumes the resampled period is representative; the episode screen and forecasts are retrospective exploratory evaluations.

Statistics and comparison conventions

Rolling windows are computed on the full active history before selecting display dates. Stress days are the bottom round-half-to-even(5% × n) SPY returns within the selected range, with ties ordered by date; IEF “up” means a strictly positive return. Sharpe and Sortino use daily units, zero risk-free rate and are not annualized. Sortino uses the root mean square of negative returns over all 126 days. Forecast periods use origin dates, and their following 21-day targets may cross a boundary. MAE comparisons need six origins; percentage changes use persistence MAE as denominator. The approximate 95% HAC interval shown on forecast cards describes the paired mean absolute-loss difference in daily-volatility percentage points, not the relative-MAE percentage. Negative favors HAR. It uses a normal 1.96 critical value; short periods have limited precision. Newey–West loss statistics use Bartlett weights with floor(4 × (n/100)^(2/9)) lags, capped at n−1; they are exploratory and do not correct for selecting models or periods.
